ERA V5 · Session 8

How attention
works now

Every mechanism in the order it was actually launched — 2014 to 2026, oldest first. Not grouped by family, not in teaching order. Chronological, because that is the only order in which you can watch the field change its mind.

Vanilla attention was never wrong. It was expensive. Every single thing that follows is somebody looking at that bill and trying to pay less of it — and each one pays with something different.

Act I · 2014–2017
It wants exactness
Attention is invented, and it is exact. Sequences are short, so nobody minds that it costs T².
Act II · 2019–2021
The bill arrives
Read fewer keys, or delete the softmax and keep a fixed state. Both trade exactness away, and both lose quality doing it.
Act III · 2021–2024
It wants length
Position becomes the wall. Rotations replace tables, then get rescaled to stretch short-trained models over long windows.
Act IV · 2023–2026
It wants memory back
Serving cost replaces training cost. Compress heads, features and sequence — and stop picking one mechanism per network.

Two things you can only see from a timeline. The order is not the teaching order — segment recurrence (2019) is older than RoPE (2021), and MQA (2019) is older than everything in Acts II and III despite being ignored for four years. And the field reverses direction twice: it gives up exactness for cost, then FlashAttention makes exactness cheap again and retires a chunk of that work.

date integrity

Three things I got wrong, or nearly did

You asked us not to trust a confident sentence. These are the places where checking changed the answer — including one where a search engine confidently attributed a technique to a paper that does not contain it.

1 · "DroPE" is three different things, and two of them are not what Session 8 meant

  1. DroPE — LLM context extension. arXiv:2512.12167, 13 Dec 2025, Gelberg, Eguchi, Akiba & Cetin at Sakana AI: "Extending the Context of Pretrained LLMs by Dropping Their Positional Embeddings". This is the one the session meant — pretrain with RoPE, then remove positional embeddings and briefly recalibrate at the original context length. Verified from the arXiv abstract page and the Sakana project page.
  2. DRoPE — autonomous driving. arXiv:2503.15029, 19 Mar 2025, "Directional Rotary Position Embedding for Efficient Agent Interaction Modeling". Adapts RoPE to encode vehicle headings for trajectory generation. Nothing to do with context length. A name collision that will mislead anyone who searches carelessly.
  3. A hallucinated attribution. A web search told me arXiv:2606.07404 ("Reversible Foundations: Training a 120B Sparse MoE", Rohan Shravan, 5 Jun 2026) describes DroPE, complete with a plausible mechanism summary. I fetched the paper. It does not mention DroPE at all. This is precisely the failure mode you warned about, and it came from a search summary rather than from me — which is worse, because it reads as sourced.

2 · YaRN's date is August, not November

  1. The arXiv submission history for 2309.00071 is v1 31 Aug 2023, v2 1 Nov 2023, v3 6 Feb 2026. Secondary sources routinely cite November, which is the revision. I had November in my head too.
  2. It matters for ordering: v1 puts YaRN before StreamingLLM (29 Sep 2023), not after. Using v2 would have put two entries in the wrong sequence — in a submission whose entire premise is the sequence.
  3. The timeline therefore uses arXiv v1 dates throughout, and the page runs an automated ascending-order check at load. Its result is printed below.

3 · One date genuinely cannot be pinned, and I have said so rather than guessing

  1. NTK-aware scaled RoPE has no paper. It is a r/LocalLLaMA post by bloc97, with a Dynamic-NTK follow-up by emozilla. There is no arXiv v1 stamp to check.
  2. Sources agree on mid-2023 and confirm it influenced CodeLlama and Qwen. The timeline places it at 28 Jun 2023, flagged ≈, and its ordering relative to PI (27 Jun 2023) and YaRN (31 Aug 2023) is reliable even though the exact day is not.
  3. Qwen3-Next's release day is similarly approximate — the month is solid, the day is taken from vLLM's day-0 support post of 11 Sep 2025.

And one thing for you to check on me. The session notes say V4 used DroPE to go from 8K to 256K, a 32× extension. DroPE is real and published, as above. But arXiv:2606.07404 — the only LightningLM paper I could find, the 120B one — reports 8K context and does not mention DroPE, DeltaNet layers, DDDGDDDG, a Memory Stream, or sparse-attention budgets anywhere. So the mechanism is verified and the combination is not: I could not find a public source for V4 specifically reaching 256K via DroPE. It may be internal, or in a paper I did not find. Worth a sentence in class either way. Separately, that paper reports "a released training loss of 1.78 at 120B scale", while the session notes describe a "1.78B seed model" — the same number in two very different roles, which is the kind of coincidence worth double-checking.

method

How the dates were checked

  • One rule: the date is the arXiv v1 submission date, read off the abstract page. Not the conference date, not the journal date, not a later revision, not a blog post about the paper.
  • For non-paper releases — Qwen3-Next, DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp, NTK-aware scaling — the public release or post date, with the source named and the entry flagged if the exact day is uncertain.
  • Every entry was fetched individually. No date on this page came from memory. Where the abstract page did not confirm a mechanism detail — sinusoidal position in the Transformer paper, Mistral's window size — the entry says so under date verification rather than quietly asserting it.
  • The ordering is checked by code, not by eye. The page asserts ascending date order at load and prints the result in the Corrections section above.

Where I stopped short. I verified titles, authors and dates from primary sources, plus whatever the abstract explicitly stated. I did not re-read every full paper, so the trade-off descriptions draw on the abstracts, the project pages and prior knowledge. If a trade-off here contradicts something you know from a paper's body, the paper wins — and please tell me, because that is the half of this I could not mechanically check.
